Abuse teaches the body to live on alert. Long after the danger is gone, the
shoulders stay up, the jaw stays tight, and the breath stays shallow. You cannot
talk a body out of that. You have to show it.

## Do this

1. Find any patch of grass, dirt, or sand. A backyard, a park, a strip beside a
   parking lot — it does not have to be beautiful.
2. Take your shoes and socks off. Stand still.
3. Feel four things, in this order: the temperature of the ground, the texture of
   it, the weight of your own body pressing down, and the air on your face.
4. Breathe in for four counts, out for six. Do that ten times. The longer exhale
   is the part that matters.
5. Before you go back in, name one thing you can see, one you can hear, and one
   you can smell.

## If it is hard

Some people feel a wave of emotion the first time they stand still outside. That
is normal, and it is not a sign you did it wrong. Put your shoes back on and go
inside whenever you want to. You can try again tomorrow.

## Why it works

Slow exhales and physical sensation both pull the nervous system out of the
braced, watchful state and back into the one where digestion, sleep, and clear
thinking happen. Five minutes will not fix everything. Five minutes a day for two
weeks changes something real.
